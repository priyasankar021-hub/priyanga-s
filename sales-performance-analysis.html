01 The Brief

Leadership needed more than a quarterly performance update — they needed to understand why the numbers looked the way they did. The team had grown, revenue had grown, but something underneath those headline numbers needed explaining: was growth genuinely healthy, or was it masking structural risk?

This analysis pulled together four years of headcount, revenue, and individual sales manager performance data to answer that question directly — and to flag the risks leadership couldn't see from the topline numbers alone. It was originally delivered as a single executive summary slide, then unpacked into deeper supporting analysis for follow-up discussion.

04 Strategic Takeaways

Pulling the individual analyses together, three structural questions emerged for leadership — each one a question the topline revenue and headcount numbers alone would never have surfaced.

1. Growth is real, but it's growth by addition, not by improvement. Revenue grew nearly 30% over four years, but that came largely from a similarly sized increase in the team — not from each person selling more. Without addressing ramp-up speed, pipeline quality, and conversion, the next year of growth will require the next round of hiring.
2. Performance is increasingly concentrated, which is a risk, not just a fact. A little over a third of the team drives close to 80% of revenue. That's not unusual in sales — but it is a sustainability and succession risk if those specific people leave, plateau, or are over-relied upon for stretch targets.
3. Target-setting itself may be part of the problem. With over half the team missing their EAV1 target in 2024, the question isn't just "who underperformed" — it's whether targets, enablement, or market conditions are misaligned for the middle tier of the team.
